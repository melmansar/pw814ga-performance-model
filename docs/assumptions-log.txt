1.3 Engine architecture

Source: EASA TCDS IM.E.096, Issue 07, Section III.2, page 7.

  Two spool, high bypass ratio turbofan.
  Low pressure spool:  fan, 2 boost stages, 5 stage low pressure
                       turbine
  High pressure spool: 8 stage axial high pressure compressor,
                       2 stage high pressure turbine
  Combustor:           straight flow annular, low emission

The overall pressure ratio of 33.56 is split across the fan (core
side), the booster and the high pressure compressor.


1.4 Exhaust layout

Source: Pratt & Whitney, PW800 product page, cutaway diagram
labeled "Mixer" and "Bypass Duct". Accessed 10 November 2026.
https://www.rtx.com/en/prattwhitney/products/business-aviation-engines/pw800

The PW814GA has a mixed exhaust: bypass air and core exhaust mix
before leaving through a single nozzle.

3. MODELING ASSUMPTIONS
-----------------------

(To be filled in as the model is built.)

* ITT is not turbine inlet temperature. TIT is higher than ITT because
  the turbine extracts work and cooling air mixes in. The 965 C limit
  is used as a check: the calibrated model's temperature at the ITT
  station at takeoff must be at or below 965 C.
* The databank point is at standard day sea level (15 C), below the
  33 C ambient limit, so the engine runs with temperature margin there.
  ITT is not set equal to 965 C at the design point.
* Fan pressure ratio is not guessed. It is solved from pressure
  balance at the mixer: the bypass and core streams must have about
  the same pressure where they meet (method from Mattingly, page to
  be cited). The split of the remaining core pressure ratio between
  the booster and the high pressure compressor is fixed at a textbook
  value with a range.
* Component efficiencies (fan, booster, compressors, turbines) are
  input as polytropic efficiencies, not isentropic. Polytropic
  efficiency describes the quality of each small compression or
  expansion step, so it stays about the same for a given technology
  level at any pressure ratio. Isentropic efficiency drops as
  pressure ratio rises, even with the same technology (Mattingly
  Example 7-6, pp. 398 to 399: fan e = 0.89 gives 88.2%, compressor
  e = 0.90 gives 84.2%). Holding polytropic efficiency fixed keeps
  the Week 4 pressure ratio trade study fair. Isentropic efficiency
  is still calculated and reported for each component.
